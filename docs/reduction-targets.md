# Analytic Reduction Targets for Literature Red-Team

Status: Pass 1  
Scope: competitors A--G in `docs/context-pack.md`  
Purpose: turn each literature family into an explicit null identity, rank test, causality constraint, or resource certificate that can falsify a candidate claim.

## Use rule

A candidate survives a family only after all three objects are written:

1. an explicit map from the candidate's measured response data to the competitor's mathematical object;
2. a theorem whose assumptions are checked line by line;
3. a held-out witness with uncertainty bars that violates the theorem or certifies a resource above the frozen budget.

A poor numerical fit alone is not a reduction obstruction.

## A. Enlarged GKSL and hidden ancilla

### Target object

For system dimension `d` and hidden dimension `D_hidden`, construct the reachable-observable space generated by the adjoint controlled Lindbladian,

```text
K = span{ O, L_u^*(O), L_v^* L_u^*(O), ... }.
```

Any finite enlarged-GKSL realization induces a finite-dimensional linear representation on this space. Under the frozen family-A budget,

```text
dim(K) <= (d D_hidden)^2,  D_hidden <= 6.
```

This is only a coarse necessary bound; complete positivity, trace preservation, the product initial state, and the absence of ancilla readout impose additional constraints.

### Kill test

The candidate is killed if an admissible `D_hidden <= 6` GKSL dilation reproduces calibration and held-out responses within the preregistered uncertainty tolerance.

### Survival certificate

Prefer one of:

- a lower bound `D_hidden > 6` from a minimal reachable-observable realization;
- infeasibility of the Choi/GKSL constraints with a dual certificate;
- a structural identity shared by every admissible product-initialized dilation and violated on held-out protocols.

Pseudomode and reaction-coordinate constructions are warnings that a finite auxiliary description may exist even when the reduced dynamics is strongly non-Markovian. Therefore, “non-Markovian” is not itself a survival argument.

## B. Tilted GKSL and full counting statistics

### Target object

For counting fields `s`, define the tilted generator `L_s` and moment-generating function

```text
Z_t(s) = Tr[ exp(t L_s)(rho_0) ],
K_t(s) = log Z_t(s).
```

Cumulants are derivatives of `K_t` at `s = 0`. With at most two fields, four counted jumps, cumulant order `k <= 4`, and protocol depth `d <= 4`, all reported witnesses must be expressible in the corresponding finite derivative hierarchy.

### Kill test

The candidate is killed if one admissible tilted-GKSL model reproduces the calibrated low-order cumulants and all held-out cumulant responses under the same parameters.

### Survival certificate

Construct a polynomial or differential identity among derivatives of `Z_t` that holds for the entire frozen tilted family, then show a statistically significant held-out violation. The identity must be invariant under harmless reparameterizations of the counting fields.

## C. Compact-group synchronization

### Pairwise target

For noiseless ratios `g_ij = g_i g_j^{-1}`, every cycle obeys

```text
g_i1i2 g_i2i3 ... g_iri1 = e.
```

Cycle-edge and compact-group synchronization methods turn approximate violations into robust estimation problems.

### Higher-order target

Competitor C must include hyperedge measurements and potentials up to the candidate's tested depth, at minimum hyperedge order `h = 3,4`. Higher-order synchronization now supplies exact compatibility conditions for general compact groups; a depth-three witness is not novel merely because pairwise edge synchronization misses it.

### Kill test

The candidate is killed if its response tensor maps to compatible group-valued hyperedge data for any frozen group and parameter budget, including the higher-order model.

### Survival certificate

Exhibit a gauge-invariant incompatibility that:

- remains after optimizing over the allowed group representation;
- cannot be repaired by an order-3 or order-4 hyperedge potential;
- has a quantitative margin larger than noise and drift uncertainty.

The response-to-group map must be explicit; analogy to holonomy is insufficient.

## D. Controlled HMM and state-space realization

### Target object

From controlled word probabilities or response coefficients, build the generalized Hankel matrix

```text
H[p, s] = Pr(prefix p followed by suffix s),
```

with controls included in the alphabet or as indexed transition operators.

For a classical hidden-state realization of dimension `D`,

```text
rank(H) <= D.
```

Finite blocks of `H` give computable lower bounds. Generic HMM theorems provide finite-length identifiability, but cancellation-protected models can lie in exceptional nongeneric sets and require a separate reachability/null-space analysis.

### Kill test

The candidate is killed if a controlled model with `D <= 6` and `P <= 256` reproduces all shared data under the preregistered selection procedure.

### Survival certificate

Use a noise-robust singular-value lower bound proving

```text
rank(H) >= 7
```

for the same response alphabet and held-out protocols, or provide an equivalent minimal-realization certificate. Report the perturbation theorem used to convert empirical singular values into a rank claim.

For quantum hidden-memory models with classical outputs, recent spectral-invariant work gives lower bounds on minimal quantum memory. Those results do not apply automatically to arbitrary quantum-valued interfaces; the mapping must be proved.

## E. Process tensor and quantum comb

### Causality target

A valid process-tensor Choi operator `Υ_{k:0}` is positive and satisfies the recursive causal trace constraints. In schematic form,

```text
Tr[out_j](Υ_{j:0}) = I[in_j] ⊗ Υ_{j-1:0}.
```

These constraints characterize allowed multi-time processes, not a novelty obstruction by themselves.

### Memory target

Across every temporal cut, reshape `Υ` and compute the operator-Schmidt rank. For an exact matrix-product-operator representation, the minimal bond dimension is the maximum relevant cut rank. Hence any exact model with frozen `chi <= 36` obeys

```text
rank_cut(Υ) <= 36
```

at every tested cut. Finite Markov order must be evaluated relative to the allowed instrument sequence; it is not generally instrument-independent.

### Kill test

The candidate is killed if a positive causal process tensor with `D_M <= 6`, `chi <= 36`, and `mu <= 3` predicts the held-out protocols within tolerance.

### Survival certificate

Prefer:

- a certified cut-rank lower bound `rank_cut >= 37`;
- an instrument-specific Markov-order lower bound `mu >= 4`;
- a memory-dimension lower bound `D_M >= 7`;
- infeasibility of the positive causal MPO constraints with a dual witness.

A large experimental reconstruction cost is not a lower bound. Reported shot counts from a particular tomography protocol cannot substitute for a minimax or information-theoretic impossibility theorem.

## F. Active experimental design

### Target object

Let `M` denote the competitor family, `q` a candidate protocol, and `D_t` the accumulated data. A standard design score is expected information gain,

```text
EIG(q) = E_y[ KL( p(M | D_t, q, y) || p(M | D_t) ) ].
```

Alternative scores are permitted only if fixed before held-out evaluation.

### Kill test

The candidate is killed if an allowed four-round policy, with at most 32 candidate protocols per round, 4096 posterior particles, 32 restarts, and total shots `<= 1.2e5`, reaches the same discriminating protocol and predictive conclusion.

### Survival certificate

A valid separation needs a lower bound applying to every admissible policy under the same observation model. Beating one Bayesian or reinforcement-learning heuristic is not sufficient.

## G. Analytic reduction gate

Every candidate must pass all applicable entries below before numerical novelty is claimed.

### Krylov and observable reduction

Compute the minimal invariant operator space for the specified initial states, controls, and observables. If exact reduction reproduces the interface, the apparent microscopic complexity is irrelevant.

### BCH and Dyson

Expand controlled protocol compositions to the first order at which the witness appears. Check whether the purported anomaly is an ordinary nested commutator, time-ordering term, or truncated-series remainder.

### Schur complement and Zeno/adiabatic elimination

Partition slow and fast sectors and compare with the appropriate effective generator. Strong-coupling or fast-dissipation theorems require spectral and semisimplicity assumptions; error scaling must be evaluated at the finite parameters used in the witness.

### Kalman and linear realization

For any linearized or Gaussian regime, compute observability, controllability, and generalized Hankel rank. A minimal linear state-space model kills claims based only on a high-dimensional microscopic presentation.

### Quantum regression

Test whether the multi-time correlators follow from the same reduced propagator and stationary state under the regression assumptions. Failure of naive regression is not sufficient if a finite hidden realization or process tensor still explains the data.

### Symmetry

Quotient exact gauge and dynamical symmetries before counting parameters or dimensions. A lower bound stated before symmetry reduction is invalid.

## Priority order for the next claim

1. Freeze one concrete candidate response functional and its alphabet.
2. Build its generalized Hankel blocks and temporal cut matrices.
3. Test higher-order compact-group compatibility at orders 3 and 4.
4. Run exact observable-Krylov reduction.
5. Only then fit the frozen competitor families.
6. Reserve depth-4 protocols strictly for held-out evaluation.

## Pass-1 conclusion

The shortest credible route is a resource lower-bound theorem, not the qualitative statement “low-depth data agree but depth-three data differ.” The present literature already contains:

- finite HMM realization and rank machinery;
- higher-order compact-group compatibility;
- unrestricted quantum-comb/process-tensor realization;
- exact observable-dependent open-system reduction.

A surviving claim should therefore certify that the same observed interface forces at least one resource above a frozen threshold, with an explicit robustness margin.
